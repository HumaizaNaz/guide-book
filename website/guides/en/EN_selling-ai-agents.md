# Selling AI Agents to Clients
*Pitch, pricing model, and handling objections — without looking cheap*

---

## Why Selling Is a Skill Too

Building the best AI agent isn't enough — if you can't **sell** it the right way, no client will hire you, no matter how good your code is. This guide is the next step after "Getting Paid": **now you know what to charge — this guide teaches you how to pitch.**

---

## PART 1 — PRICING MODELS (Which One, When)

```
Model                  │ How It Works                      │ Best For
────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────
Fixed project fee       │ One-time $X, full agent delivered  │ MVP, one-off builds
Setup fee + monthly     │ $X setup + $Y/month maintenance    │ Ongoing service
Usage-based             │ Base fee + per-conversation/action │ High-volume agents
Retainer                │ Fixed monthly, unlimited support   │ Long-term clients
```

**Real example:** "$150/month base + $0.50 per conversation handled by the AI" — this model aligns with the client's outcome. The more they use it, the more you earn — both incentives match.

### Two Numbers That Matter

1. **Your floor** — platform/API cost per client + your time cost. Never price below this.
2. **The client's anchor** — how much the problem currently costs them (manual labor, lost leads, slow response time).

**Always set your price between these two — closer to the client's anchor, not your floor.**

---

## PART 2 — THE PITCH (Structure)

```
1. Demo first, talk later
   → "Look what I built on your actual data" beats
     any slide deck

2. Speak their language, not your tools'
   → The client doesn't want a "RAG pipeline," they want
     "instant answers to customer questions"

3. Build a narrow demo on their real content
   → Before the meeting, build a small working demo
     from their actual FAQ/documents

4. Give a number they can approve without a meeting
   → Simple, clear pricing — no confusing tiers
```

---

## PART 3 — COMMON OBJECTIONS (And How to Handle Them)

```
Objection: "This is expensive"
→ Reframe: "This isn't a cost, it's an investment — how much
  time/money does this currently take to handle manually?"

Objection: "We'll just build it ourselves"
→ "You absolutely can — but I'll deliver it in 2 weeks,
  what your team would build in 2 months"

Objection: "AI can give wrong answers"
→ Show guardrails and human handoff — explain when the
  agent escalates to a human

Objection: "Why a setup fee?"
→ You can discount the setup fee for a long-term monthly
  retainer commitment — but don't make it entirely free
```

---

## PART 4 — AFTER THE FIRST 10 SALES

Your first clients were priced when you'd never delivered before. Now you have proof:

```
✓ Build case studies (with before/after metrics)
✓ Raise your rates — you can grandfather old clients in
✓ Move toward a subscription/retainer model
   (a one-off build's margin is earned once,
   a monthly retainer compounds)
```

---

## PART 5 — MVP Pricing Reference

```
Typical MVP (2-3 months, fixed budget agreed in advance):
  ~$10,000 for a working custom AI agent

This is the benchmark you can start negotiating from —
simple automation less, complex multi-agent systems more.
```
